## The problem

Campus shuttles ran on a printed timetable that was wrong about half the time. Students waited in the cold without knowing whether the bus was two minutes out or had already passed. The vendor's tracking app refreshed once a minute and crashed on older phones.

## The approach

I split the system into three pieces, so each could fail without taking down the others:

- **Ingest (Go):** a small service accepts GPS pings from each bus's tablet over HTTPS, validates them, and writes them to PostgreSQL.
- **Fan-out:** PostgreSQL `LISTEN/NOTIFY` pushes new positions to a server-sent-events endpoint. No message broker is needed at this scale.
- **Client (Next.js):** a statically rendered map that opens one SSE stream and interpolates bus positions between pings.

```go
// Reject pings that would teleport a bus (bad GPS fix).
if dist(prev, ping) / ping.Since(prev).Seconds() > maxSpeedMPS {
    metrics.Dropped.Inc()
    return ErrImplausible
}
```

## Challenges

GPS drift near tall buildings made buses "jump" across the quad. Filtering on physically plausible speed removed most of it. A 5-point moving average on heading handled the rest.

> The hardest part wasn't the code. It was getting 40 drivers to leave the tablet plugged in.

## Results

Weekly riders grew from about 600 in the first month to over 4,000. The transportation office now funds hosting, and two more campuses have asked to run it.

## What I'd do differently

I'd add ETA predictions earlier. They turned out to be the feature people cared about most, and they need weeks of historical data before they get good.
